Privacy Policy

Fashion Factory itself collects no personal data; the Google ads it shows do, as described below. There is no account to create, no analytics and no tracking: the app never asks permission to track you. Everything you enter — your orders, their sizes, prices, return dates and whether you kept them — stays on your device and is not sent to us. Return reminders are scheduled on your device too. The app shows ads from Google AdMob. Google may use device identifiers to serve and measure those ads; see Advertising below.

Effective 14 October 2026. Applies to the Fashion Factory mobile app for Android and iOS.

What the app stores on your device

Fashion Factory keeps two things in your device's own local storage (SharedPreferences on Android, NSUserDefaults on iOS). That is the complete list of what the app itself stores:

WhatWhyStored as
Your orders So your size at each brand, and every return-by date, survive closing the app fashionfactory.orders.v1 — for each order: brand, category, size as typed (and shoe size system), optional cut note, store, optional product link, optional price and currency, order date, return-by date, kept / returned / exchanged and the reason, the date you recorded it and, for an exchange, which order it replaced
Your settings So the app remembers your choices fashionfactory.settings.v1 — whether the introduction was shown, reminder timing (3 days, 1 day or off), default currency, and whether the reminder explanation and your phone's notification-permission prompt have been shown
Scheduled reminders To remind you before a return window closes Held by your phone's own notification scheduler (not by the app's storage): at most two per order, cancelled when you record a verdict or delete the order

Earlier versions stored a saved-products list, alert switches and a category under keys beginning fashionfactory.v2. and styleradar.v1.. Version 3.0.0 deletes those keys the first time it opens; nothing is carried across.

None of it identifies you. There is no name, email address, phone number, location, contact list, photo, advertising identifier or device identifier involved, because the app never asks for any of those. This data never leaves your device, and we cannot see it.

Google's consent SDK, part of the ads described below, also keeps your ad consent choice in the same local storage, under the industry-standard IABTCF_ keys, so you are not asked again on every launch.

What the app never does

  • No account, sign-up or login.
  • No analytics or usage measurement of any kind.
  • No advertising identifier from us: the app never shows the iOS App Tracking Transparency prompt, and on Android it removes the advertising-ID permission.
  • No tracking across apps or websites, and no data shared with data brokers.
  • No crash-reporting service. If the app hits an error it is written to the device's own log and goes no further.
  • No third-party analytics, attribution or social SDKs. The app's entire set of third-party components is: a local-storage plugin, a local-notifications plugin with its time-zone database, a component that opens links in your browser, a component that opens your phone's share sheet, a component that opens the app's page in system settings, an icon font, and Google's Mobile Ads SDK, which shows the ads described below. The component that connects the app to the ads SDK also brings Google's Firebase libraries (Analytics, Crashlytics, Performance Monitoring and Remote Config) with it. The app's code never calls them, and their analytics, crash-reporting and performance collection is switched off in the app's configuration.
  • No sale of personal information — there is none to sell.

Advertising

Fashion Factory shows ads from Google AdMob: one banner at the end of the home screen and one at the end of a brand page. There are no full-screen ads, and no ads on the order form or in Settings. Ads are served by Google, not by us; we receive only aggregate reports from Google, nothing that identifies you.

To serve and measure ads, Google's Mobile Ads SDK sends Google information such as your device type, operating system, approximate location derived from your IP address, ad interactions, and device identifiers. The app does not request App Tracking Transparency on iOS and removes the advertising-ID permission on Android, so the advertising identifier on either platform is not available to it. How Google uses this information is described in Google's own privacy policy, at policies.google.com/privacy.

In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, Google's consent form asks for your choice before any ad is requested, and a Privacy choices row in Settings, always visible, lets you change it at any time.

Network activity

Fashion Factory's own features make no network requests: orders, sizes and reminders never leave your device. The only network activity is the Google ads described above and, on iPhone and iPad, one request the first time the app opens: the Firebase libraries described above register a random installation identifier with Google (firebaseinstallations.googleapis.com). Nothing you enter is sent with it, and their analytics, crash-reporting and performance collection stays switched off.

If you paste a product link into an order, the store's name is recognised from the link's address on your device; the page is never requested. Tapping Open on a saved link, or Privacy policy in Settings, hands that address to your own browser and leaves Fashion Factory. From that point you are on that website, under its privacy policy.

The app refuses unencrypted connections outright.

Permissions

On Android the app requests INTERNET (used only by the ads above) and RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED (so your return reminders are put back after the phone restarts). The notifications component adds POST_NOTIFICATIONS — on Android 13 and later you are asked when you first save an order with a return date, after a short explanation in the app (or later, from Settings) — and VIBRATE. Google's Mobile Ads SDK and the Firebase libraries described above add permissions that are granted without a prompt: ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE, WAKE_LOCK, FOREGROUND_SERVICE, Google Play's install-referrer permission and Android's ad-services permissions (ACCESS_ADSERVICES_AD_ID, ACCESS_ADSERVICES_ATTRIBUTION, ACCESS_ADSERVICES_TOPICS); the advertising-ID permission (AD_ID) is removed. On iOS the app asks only for permission to show notifications, at the same moment, and never shows the tracking prompt. No location, camera, contacts, storage, microphone or phone permissions.

Children

Fashion Factory is an app for keeping track of your own clothing orders, intended for a general audience, not directed at children. The app itself collects no personal information from anyone, and therefore none from children.

Your choices and your data

  • Delete an order — Delete on the order removes it and its reminders.
  • Turn reminders off — Settings → Return reminders → Off, or in your phone's notification settings.
  • Export or share — Settings → Export orders (CSV) and Share my sizes hand a file or text to your share sheet; you choose where it goes.
  • Change your ad consent — where Google's consent form applies, the Privacy choices row in Settings reopens it.
  • Delete everything — uninstalling the app removes your orders, your settings and any scheduled reminders, and the stored ad consent choice, from your device. Settings → Delete all data removes your orders, your settings and any scheduled reminders without uninstalling. We hold nothing anywhere else, so there is no request you need to make of us. Data Google collects for ads is governed by Google's privacy policy.

Changes to this policy

If the app's behaviour changes in a way that affects privacy — a crash-reporting service, an account system, anything that collects data — this policy will be updated before that change ships, and the effective date above will change with it. The policy lives in the app's own source repository alongside the code it describes, and an automated check fails the build if a data-collecting dependency is added while this page still says there is none.
